# Atlas of Quiet Explorers — Fact Check & Data Verification Ledger

This document audits and tracks scientific facts, coordinate frames, operational statuses, and corrections against official NASA and NSSDCA primary sources.

---

## 1. Primary Source Audits & Discrepancy Corrections

### [A1] Apollo 11 Lunar Module / EASEP (NSSDCA ID: 1969-059C)
- **Landed**: 20 July 1969 at 20:17:40 UT.
- **Coordinates**: `0.67416° N, 23.47314° E` (Mare Tranquillitatis, DE 421 mean Earth/polar rotation-axis reference frame derived from LRO).
- **Descent Stage vs. Ascent Stage**:
  - The **Descent Stage** served as the landing rocket and launch platform; it remains stationary on the lunar surface at Tranquility Base.
  - The **Ascent Stage** was jettisoned after rendezvous in lunar orbit; its fate is unknown, but presumed to have impacted the lunar surface within 1–4 months.
  - *Correction applied*: Do not assign ascent stage uncertainty to the descent stage.
- **EASEP Seismometer**: Solar-powered Early Apollo Scientific Experiment Package deployed ~17 m south of the LM. Turned on 21 July 1969 (04:40:39 UT). Ceased accepting commands on **27 August 1969** (not the 1977 ALSEP network shutdown).
- **Laser Ranging Retroreflector (LRRR)**: Deployed ~5 m NNW of the LM. Passive quartz prism array requiring no electrical power.

### [A2] Apollo 12 Lunar Module / ALSEP & Surveyor 3 (NSSDCA ID: 1969-099C)
- **Landed**: 19 November 1969 at 06:54:35 UT.
- **Coordinates**: `3.0128° S, 23.4219° W` (Oceanus Procellarum, LRO DE 421 frame).
- **Co-location with Surveyor 3**: Landed approximately **180 m** from Surveyor 3. Astronauts Pete Conrad and Alan Bean retrieved ~10 kg of Surveyor parts (camera, scoop, tubing) during their second EVA.
- **ALSEP Station**: Powered by a SNAP-27 RTG. Switched on 19 November 1969 (14:21 UT), decommissioned 30 September 1977. ALSEP coordinates: `3.0094° S, 23.4246° W` (distinct from LM pad).
- *Correction applied*: Apollo 11, Apollo 12, and Apollo 15 landing sites are completely separate locations on the Moon. Apollo 12 visited Surveyor 3; Apollo 11 did not.

### [A3] Apollo 15 Lunar Module / ALSEP / LRV (NSSDCA ID: 1971-063C)
- **Landed**: 30 July 1971 at 22:16:29 UT.
- **Coordinates**: `26.13239° N, 3.63330° E` (Hadley-Apennine, Mare Imbrium).
- **Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV-1)**:
  - Mass: 210 kg. Two 36-volt non-rechargeable silver-zinc batteries.
  - Total driving distance: **27.8 km** (rover odometer) / **27.9 km** (total surface activity). Max distance from LM: 5.0 km.
  - Samples collected: 77.31 kg of rock and soil.
- **Mass Clarification**: The NSSDCA record reports 16,430 kg as the initial total LM launch mass (including propellant and ascent stage), not the discarded descent stage alone.

### [A4] Surveyor 3 (NASA Science / NSSDCA ID: 1967-035A)
- **Landed**: 20 April 1967 at 00:04:17 UT.
- **Coordinates**: `3.01° S, 23.41° W` (Oceanus Procellarum).
- **Operations**: TV camera returned 6,326 images. Surface sampler arm dug 4 trenches (up to 18 cm deep) and performed 13 impact tests. Demonstrated that lunar regolith could structurally support the Apollo Lunar Module. Contact lost 4 May 1967 following lunar nightfall.

### [A5] Mars Pathfinder & Sojourner (Carl Sagan Memorial Station)
- **Landed**: 4 July 1997 in **Ares Vallis** (`19.33° N, 33.55° W` regional floodplain).
- **Engineering**: Parachute and airbag deceleration system; lander bounced ~15 times before resting.
- **Sojourner Rover**: Mass 10.6 kg. 6-wheel rocker-bogie chassis. APXS spectrometer identified high-silica and volcanic andesitic rocks. Rounded pebbles provided evidence of catastrophic ancient flooding. Last data transmission: 27 September 1997.

### [A6] Spirit and Opportunity (Mars Exploration Rovers)
- **Spirit (MER-A)**: Landed 3 January 2004 in Gusev Crater (Columbia Memorial Station). Mission ended in 2010 after 7.7 km.
- **Opportunity (MER-B)**: Landed 24 January 2004 in Meridiani Planum (opposite hemisphere from Spirit). Explored Eagle, Endurance, Victoria, and Endeavour craters. Last contact: 10 June 2018 during global dust storm after driving **45.16 km**.
- *Correction applied*: Spirit and Opportunity were on opposite sides of Mars, never co-located.

### [A7] Viking 1 Lander (Thomas Mutch Memorial Station)
- **Landed**: 20 July 1976 at 11:53:06 UT.
- **Coordinates**: `22.483° N, 47.94° W` (Chryse Planitia).
- **Scientific Findings**: Returned 4,500+ images and meteorology logs. Biology experiments (Labeled Release, Pyrolytic Release, Gas Exchange) gave ambiguous results, while Gas Chromatograph Mass Spectrometer (GCMS) found no organic compounds. **The mission did not establish the presence of life.**
- **Retirement**: Commanded accidentally interrupted on 11 November 1982 by an erroneous battery charging uplink; attempts to restore communications ended in 1983.

### [A8] InSight Mars Lander
- **Landed**: 26 November 2018 in **Elysium Planitia** (~`4.5° N, 135.9° E`).
- **Science Return**: SEIS seismometer detected **more than 1,300 marsquakes**, revealing crust thickness (24–72 km), liquid metallic core, and mantle structure.
- *Correction applied*: The Heat Flow and Physical Properties Package (HP3 "Mole") did not achieve its intended 3–5 meter depth due to soil friction resistance, penetrator friction limits, and hard duricrust. Mission ended 15 December 2022 due to dust accumulation on solar arrays.

### [A9] Dawn (Main Asteroid Belt)
- **Launched**: 27 September 2007.
- **Vesta Visit**: Entered orbit 16 July 2011; departed **5 September 2012** (corrected from old prompt claim of 2017).
- **Ceres Visit**: Entered Ceres orbit **6 March 2015**. Communications ceased 31 October 2018 when hydrazine attitude thrusters depleted.
- *Status*: Passive orbital retention around dwarf planet Ceres. Planetary protection protocols predict a stable orbit for several hundred years; no "perpetual" or "forever" claims are made.

### [A10] NEAR Shoemaker (Asteroid 433 Eros)
- **Orbit & Touchdown**: Entered orbit around 433 Eros on 14 February 2000. Made a gentle controlled descent at ~1.8 m/s on **12 February 2001** just south of Himeros saddle depression.
- **Surface Science**: Although not designed as a lander, its X-ray/Gamma-ray spectrometer returned composition data from the surface until 28 February 2001.

### [A11] Pioneer 10 (Interstellar Trajectory)
- **Launched**: 2 March 1972. First spacecraft to fly past Jupiter (4 December 1973).
- **Last Signal**: **23 January 2003** from a distance of **12.23 billion km (81.8 AU)** with an 11 hr 20 min light travel time.
- *Correction applied*: Pioneer 10 is on an escape trajectory heading toward Aldebaran (Taurus). It is not being actively tracked in real time, and no verified crossing of the heliopause has been announced for Pioneer 10.

### [A12] Ingenuity Mars Helicopter
- **Landed**: 18 February 2021 with Perseverance in Jezero Crater.
- **Flight Record**: Completed **72 flights** (designed for 5 technology demonstrations), accumulating **128.8 minutes of airtime** and **17.0 km** ground distance, reaching 24 m altitude.
- **Mission Conclusion**: Retired 25 January 2024 following rotor tip strike and blade damage during Flight 72 landing on 18 January 2024. Station remains silent on the Jezero Crater plains.

### [A13] Kepler Space Telescope
- **Launched**: 6 March 2009.
- **Orbit**: Earth-trailing heliocentric orbit (not Moon or Mars).
- **Science Legacy**: Discovered **over 2,600 confirmed exoplanets** using the transit photometry technique.
- **Mission Retirement**: Decommissioned 30 October 2018 after depleting onboard reaction control fuel.
